developer · anthropic.com

Anthropic ditches Git for custom 'Scion' system

What happened: Anthropic has migrated its main monorepo to a custom version control system called Scion after daily code merges surged from 2,500 to over 30,000, driven by autonomous coding agents.

Why it matters: It is a practical look at how AI-driven development is outgrowing standard industry tools, forcing companies to build bespoke infrastructure just to keep up with their own agents.

Our read: When your AI agents start treating your version control system like a stress test, you know the workflow has fundamentally shifted. It is a reminder that the 'AI-native' stack is still being written in real-time.

business · Ryan McMorrow/Financial Times

CoreWeave eyes insurance capital for GPU debt

What happened: CoreWeave is seeking investment-grade credit ratings on its chip-collateralized loans to access capital from global insurance funds, following $35 billion in 2026 fundraising and ~$52 billion in total obligations.

Why it matters: The sheer scale of AI infrastructure spending is pushing companies to tap into new, massive pools of institutional capital, moving beyond traditional venture or bank debt.

Our read: The race to own the hardware layer of AI is becoming a massive exercise in financial engineering. Whether insurance funds are the right home for this specific risk remains an open question for credit analysts.

technology · Yuliia Dysa/Reuters

Starlink speed caps disrupt Ukrainian drone missions

What happened: SpaceX has implemented an unannounced 100 mph speed restriction on Starshield terminals in Ukraine, which military units report has caused connectivity drops during active drone and air-defense missions.

Why it matters: This highlights the precarious reliance on commercial satellite networks for modern military operations, where a software-defined limit can have immediate, kinetic consequences.

Our read: The lack of official communication from SpaceX leaves frontline operators guessing. It is a stark example of how a private company's service terms can inadvertently dictate the operational tempo of a conflict.

research · Reuters

Nvidia pledges $250M in compute for UK AI safety

What happened: Nvidia has committed $250 million in accelerated computing resources over five years to support UK AI safety research, specifically targeting agentic security and foundation model safety at institutions like Oxford and Imperial College London.

Why it matters: This provides a significant, albeit non-cash, boost to UK-based research labs, focusing on the technical challenges of securing increasingly autonomous AI systems.

Our read: It is a strategic move to keep the UK's safety research ecosystem well-supplied with the hardware it needs to test the very models Nvidia is helping to build.
