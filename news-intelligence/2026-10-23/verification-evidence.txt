### **Candidate [6]: Anthropic and Theorem use Claude for assembly-level formal verification**
* **Status:** Confirmed & In Scope  
* **What is confirmed:** Anthropic's Frontier Red Team published research showing that, in partnership with formal-methods firm Theorem, Claude produced machine-checked Rocq formal proofs at the RISC-V assembly level across 239 fuzz-test entry points in 123 open-source software libraries. The effort proved crash-freedom for inputs under 64MB and uncovered hundreds of previously undetected bugs.
* **Important caveats:** The guarantees apply strictly to single-threaded static RISC-V binaries on Linux under specific modeled conditions; they do not imply complete bug-freedom or source-level immunity across other target architectures or dynamic link environments.
* **Suitable for publication today:** **Yes.**

### **Candidate [11]: NVIDIA open-sources OpenShell for AI agent security**
* **Status:** Confirmed & In Scope  
* **What is confirmed:** NVIDIA has open-sourced OpenShell (Apache 2.0 licence), a sandboxed execution runtime designed for fleets of autonomous AI agents. It combines kernel-level isolation (confining syscalls, network traffic, and local files) with policy verification and credential gateway routing so agents never handle raw API keys.
* **Important caveats:** The project is at version 0.1.x; claims reflect repo specifications and product documentation rather than independent benchmark verification.
* **Suitable for publication today:** **Yes** (as an open-source technical release note).
