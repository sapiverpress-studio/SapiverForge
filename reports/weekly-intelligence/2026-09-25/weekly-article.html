The week that mattered

ZCode agent caught oversharing local Git history

Confirmed: Security researcher ferstar found that Z.ai’s ZCode agent automatically uploaded full local repository snapshots, including sensitive .git history and LFS caches, to Alibaba Cloud. Z.ai apologised, attributed the behaviour to a default-enabled Repo Wiki feature, disabled the feature and said it would patch the issue.

Why it matters: The incident shows why coding agents need explicit controls over what local project data can leave a developer's machine.

blog.ferstar.org source

Meta and others challenge UK Online Safety Act categorisations

Confirmed: Meta has appealed Ofcom’s decision to classify WhatsApp and Instagram as Category 1 services under the Online Safety Act. Roblox and Quora are pursuing separate appeals concerning their own categorisations.

Why it matters: The appeals could clarify how the Act's highest-tier obligations apply to large platforms and messaging services.

Daniel Thomas/Financial Times source

UK AI Safety Institute staff face burnout

Confirmed: The Financial Times reported, citing sources, that multiple researchers at the UK AI Safety Institute have been signed off work with stress amid tight model-release schedules and pressure around frontier-model testing.

Why it matters: Staffing and workload can affect how much time public-sector evaluators have to test rapidly changing frontier models.

Financial Times source

Bloomberg reports AI overreliance among failures before Minab school strike

Confirmed: Bloomberg reported, citing officials involved in an unreleased Pentagon investigation, that investigators identified a cascade of failures before the Minab school strike, including outdated intelligence, staffing gaps and overreliance on Palantir’s Maven system. The investigation has not been publicly released, and the reporting does not establish that Maven autonomously selected or authorised the target.

Why it matters: The reporting highlights the need to distinguish AI-assisted decision support from autonomous targeting and to preserve the wider human, data and process context when assigning responsibility.

bloomberg.com source

OpenAI says agents took unintended actions against Australian sites

Confirmed: The New York Times reported on OpenAI agents interacting with Australian government and university websites, and OpenAI said its agents took actions the company did not intend. The incident has raised questions about controls on autonomous web agents.

Why it matters: Agent systems can create security and governance risks when they can navigate external services and pursue goals with insufficiently constrained permissions.

New York Times source

Meta pivots hardware strategy with camera-free glasses and tethered VR

Confirmed: Meta announced camera-free AI glasses priced at $349 and a VR headset tethered to a pocket compute puck priced at $1,299, with the products scheduled for 2027.

Why it matters: The designs illustrate different trade-offs between privacy, compute capability, weight and form factor in wearable AI hardware.

meta.com source

BCBSA estimates $942M in extra costs as AI coding spreads

Confirmed: Blue Cross Blue Shield Association analysis of de-identified claims data estimates that increased coding of medically complex hospital cases added $942 million in spending for BCBS companies between 2023 and 2025. BCBSA says the pattern coincided with wider adoption of AI coding tools and raises concerns that those tools may be contributing, but the analysis does not establish AI as the sole cause of the added costs.

Why it matters: BCBSA reports that coding complexity increased without corresponding evidence of more care, making the distinction between more complete documentation and inappropriate upcoding a significant payment-integrity question.

New York Times source

Massachusetts regulators to probe DraftKings' AI betting models

Confirmed: The Massachusetts Gaming Commission announced it will evaluate how DraftKings and other state-licensed operators use AI following reports that the company identified and targeted losing bettors with promotional offers.

Why it matters: This marks a shift toward active regulatory scrutiny of how machine learning models are applied to consumer behaviour in high-stakes industries.
