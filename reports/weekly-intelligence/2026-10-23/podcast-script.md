# The Infrastructure Reality Check

This week, the AI industry hit a wall—literally. From power grid constraints in Essex to the massive physical costs of data centre coatings, we look at why the 'AI boom' is increasingly a story about industrial supply chains, not just software.

## Opening

Hello, I'm Isla. This week, the narrative shifted from 'what can AI do' to 'what does it actually take to keep the lights on.' We’ve seen a week where the unglamorous, physical reality of the AI revolution—power, silicon, and infrastructure—finally caught up with the hype.

## The Power Grid Bottleneck

The most telling story this week wasn't a new model launch, but a cancelled one. Microsoft walked away from a major data centre deal in Essex because the local grid simply can't provide the power until the 2030s. It’s a stark reminder that even with billions in capital, you can't conjure electricity out of thin air. Meanwhile, the industry is betting on a massive 2027 capacity surge, but with construction delays and grid constraints, that timeline looks increasingly optimistic.

## The Cost of Doing Business

The 'picks and shovels' of the AI era are getting expensive. We’re seeing companies like PPG Industries report a tripling in demand for specialised data centre coatings—the fire and static protection that costs millions per facility. At the same time, credit markets are getting nervous; bond yields for AI infrastructure providers are spiking, suggesting investors are finally asking if these massive, capital-intensive builds will ever actually pay for themselves.

## Agents and the Security Gap

As we move toward autonomous agents, the security risks are becoming painfully cheap to exploit. A hacker recently breached over 100 entities using AI agents for just $5,000. In response, Nvidia has released OpenShell to help isolate these agents, but it’s early days. We’re also seeing Anthropic use AI to find bugs in open-source code, which is genuinely impressive, though it’s worth remembering that 'crash-free' in a lab is a long way from a secure production system.

## Corporate Consolidation and Legal Friction

Disney’s move to acquire talent from the bankrupt Character.AI is a masterclass in modern corporate strategy: why build from scratch when you can pick up the pieces in a distressed asset sale? Meanwhile, Meta has been hit with a record $25 billion penalty in New Mexico over historical privacy practices. It’s a reminder that while labs race toward AGI, the legal and regulatory baggage of the last decade isn't going anywhere.

## Closing

Looking ahead, keep an eye on the UK's grid capacity and the upcoming CMA consultation on Apple's payment ecosystem. The tech industry is currently trying to build a future that the physical world isn't quite ready to support. Until next week, stay curious.
