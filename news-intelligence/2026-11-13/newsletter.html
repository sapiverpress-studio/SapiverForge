Jensen Huang Resigns as Nvidia CEO

What happened: Nvidia announced the resignation of co-founder Jensen Huang as President, CEO, and board member on November 12, 2026, following public revelations of extramarital affairs. CFO Colette Kress has been named Interim CEO while the board initiates a search for a permanent successor.

Why it matters: The departure of a long-standing leader at the centre of the AI hardware boom creates significant uncertainty for the company's strategic direction and market stability.

Our read: The sudden exit, compounded by the nature of the allegations and the involvement of figures linked to foreign intelligence, suggests a period of intense internal and external scrutiny for Nvidia.

Microsoft 365 Cloud Services Suffer Major Outage

What happened: Microsoft 365 services, including Teams, Outlook, and Copilot AI, experienced a multi-region outage starting November 12, 2026, due to automated health check failures that caused server overloads. Microsoft has reported gradual recovery but full restoration remains ongoing.

Why it matters: The failure of core infrastructure components highlights the fragility of heavily automated cloud environments, impacting productivity for users globally.

Our read: While Microsoft has ruled out a cyberattack, the incident serves as a reminder that even highly integrated cloud systems are susceptible to cascading failures when automated safeguards malfunction.

OpenAI Launches Agentic Security Tools

What happened: OpenAI has introduced Daybreak Red Team, an early-access penetration testing service, and Daybreak Defense Factory, a system designed to identify and draft patches for code vulnerabilities. Patches generated by the defense tool require human engineer approval before deployment.

Why it matters: These tools represent a shift toward using AI agents for active security maintenance, though the reliance on human oversight for patching underscores the current limitations in automated reliability.

Our read: OpenAI is positioning its agents as force multipliers for security teams, but the requirement for human verification suggests the company is not yet confident in fully autonomous remediation.

Samsung in Talks to Acquire Data Center Cooling Firm Modine

What happened: Samsung Electronics is reportedly in discussions to acquire US-based data center cooling systems manufacturer Modine Manufacturing. No definitive agreement has been signed, and Samsung has not confirmed the report.

Why it matters: As AI data centres demand increasingly intensive cooling solutions, hardware giants are looking to secure critical infrastructure components to support their expansion.

Our read: This move suggests that the physical constraints of AI compute—specifically heat management—are becoming as strategically important as the chips themselves.
