Today we are looking at the physical and digital frontlines of the AI boom, from a data centre strike in Russia to the quiet, expensive machinery of quantum computing. Also, Amazon has finally decided that 'Fire' is a bit too 2011 for its tablets.

lead · Reuters

Ukrainian drone strike hits Yandex data centre

What happened: A Ukrainian drone attack overnight struck a major Yandex data centre in Sasovo, Russia, causing a fire and forcing a total suspension of site operations. Yandex reported that core consumer services remain unaffected, though Yandex Cloud experienced power supply disruptions.

Why it matters: This marks the first confirmed strike on a major Russian data hub during the war, raising questions about the vulnerability of critical AI and cloud infrastructure in conflict zones.

Our read: The incident highlights how physical infrastructure is increasingly becoming a target in modern digital warfare, potentially impacting the training capacity of Russian AI models if the site's supercomputing hardware sustained damage.

technology · Ashley Campion/CrowdStrike

CrowdStrike links bank cyberattacks to AI-powered agent

What happened: CrowdStrike analysis confirms that cyberattacks against South Korean financial institutions used an open-source Chinese agentic penetration testing tool called ARTEX alongside LLMs. Exfiltrated logs from the attack included prompt histories where the actor requested a security resume.

Why it matters: The use of agentic tools in active financial cyberattacks demonstrates a shift toward more automated, sophisticated threat vectors that can navigate security environments with minimal human intervention.

Our read: The presence of 'security resume' prompts in the logs suggests the attacker may have been using AI to help refine their methodology or bypass specific defences in real-time.

business · David Pierce/The Verge

Amazon retires Fire brand for 'Alexa Tablets'

What happened: Amazon has retired its 15-year-old 'Fire' tablet brand in favour of a new 'Alexa Tablet' line running Android with full Google Play access. The flagship $499 Alexa Tablet 12 Pro launches on October 14.

Why it matters: Moving away from the restrictive Fire OS to stock Android with Google Play integration is a significant pivot for Amazon's hardware strategy, likely aimed at making its devices more appealing to users who want a standard tablet experience.

Our read: It seems Amazon has finally accepted that fighting the Google Play ecosystem is a battle they would rather stop winning, or at least stop fighting.

research · Isabelle Bousquette/Wall Street Journal

Quantum startup Oratomic hits $5.4B valuation

What happened: Pasadena-based quantum startup Oratomic has raised a $475M Series B round, pushing its valuation to $5.4B. The company is developing neutral-atom quantum computing systems.

Why it matters: The rapid valuation surge from $1.5B in July to $5.4B today underscores the intense capital flowing into quantum hardware, despite the fact that fault-tolerant systems remain in the development phase.

Our read: Investors are clearly betting heavily on the long-term potential of neutral-atom architectures, though the gap between current valuations and commercial-grade quantum reliability remains substantial.
