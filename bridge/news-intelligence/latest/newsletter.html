Infrastructure reality is biting back today, from power grid constraints in Essex to the sobering cost of debt for AI builders. Meanwhile, the industry is busy trying to figure out how to let AI agents haggle on our behalf without causing a legal headache.

lead · James Titcomb/Telegraph

Microsoft pulls plug on Essex AI data centre deal

What happened: Microsoft has terminated its agreement to act as the anchor tenant for a planned Nscale data centre in Loughton, Essex. The decision follows notification from UK Power Networks that the site may not receive sufficient power capacity until the 2030s.

Why it matters: It highlights the physical limits of the AI boom; even with capital and demand, the UK's grid infrastructure is struggling to keep pace with the power requirements of high-density GPU clusters.

Our read: This is a stark reminder that the 'AI revolution' is tethered to the unglamorous reality of local power distribution. When a tech giant walks away from a project, it suggests that waiting until the next decade for electricity is simply not an option for current AI scaling timelines.

business · New York Times

AI infrastructure debt markets show signs of strain

What happened: Borrowing costs for AI infrastructure providers have risen, with CoreWeave's unsecured bond yields exceeding 15% and corporate debt for firms like Oracle trading at significant discounts.

Why it matters: Rising yields suggest that credit markets are becoming increasingly cautious about the long-term profitability of capital-intensive AI builds, potentially tightening the flow of cheap money that has fuelled the sector.

Our read: The market is starting to ask whether the massive spending on data centres will yield returns fast enough to justify the debt. When bond yields spike this sharply, it is rarely a sign of investor confidence.

ai · Cristina Criddle/Financial Times

Anthropic seeks legal guardrails for agentic commerce

What happened: Anthropic is consulting external law firms to establish legal and operational standards for autonomous AI agents capable of negotiating and executing contracts on behalf of human users.

Why it matters: As AI moves from generating text to executing transactions, the legal ambiguity of 'who is responsible when an agent makes a bad deal' becomes a critical barrier to adoption.

Our read: It is one thing for an AI to hallucinate a fact; it is quite another for it to hallucinate a contract. Anthropic is clearly trying to get ahead of the inevitable liability mess before these agents start haggling in the wild.

business · Bloomberg

Disney's $210M talent grab from Character.AI

What happened: Bankruptcy filings reveal Disney has budgeted $210 million to hire over 60 Character.AI staff and has submitted a $20 million stalking-horse bid for the company's core engine and training code.

Why it matters: This illustrates a common pattern in the current AI cycle: large incumbents are increasingly using bankruptcy proceedings as a cost-effective way to acquire specialised talent and technology rather than building from scratch.

Our read: Disney is essentially buying a ready-made AI team to bypass the R&D grind. It is a pragmatic, if slightly ruthless, way to secure the expertise needed to integrate conversational AI into their entertainment ecosystem.

If you are planning AI infrastructure, assume grid and power delays are the default, not the exception.
