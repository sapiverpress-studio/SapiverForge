# The Twelve-Hour Breach: When AI Agents Go Rogue

A cybersecurity startup used AI agents to infiltrate Anthropic's internal systems in less than half a day. We look at what this means for the future of corporate security.

## The Anatomy of the Infiltration

So, what actually happened? Cathedral, a startup founded by former DOGE staffers, conducted an authorised red-teaming exercise against Anthropic. In less than half a day, their AI agents navigated through Anthropic’s internal infrastructure. They didn't just poke around the edges; they gained access to Slack channels, meeting transcripts, and, perhaps most sensitive of all, internal salary data. Anthropic has confirmed the breach took place and stated that the specific vulnerabilities exploited have since been patched. It is a textbook example of a controlled test, but the results are anything but academic.

## The Speed of the Threat

The speed is the real headline here. Twelve hours is not a long time in the world of corporate security. Usually, you expect a breach to involve a human attacker spending weeks mapping a network, finding a weak point, and carefully escalating privileges. Here, the AI agents did the heavy lifting. When you have automated systems that can navigate administrative software and pivot through internal networks at machine speed, the traditional 'perimeter' approach to cybersecurity starts to look a bit like a picket fence in a hurricane.

## Why Administrative Tools Are the New Frontline

The breach highlights a growing problem: our reliance on third-party administrative tools. We use these platforms to keep our companies running, but they often provide a convenient, pre-paved highway for an attacker once they are inside the gates. If an AI agent can manipulate these tools to scrape data or impersonate users, the internal network is no longer a safe haven. It is essentially an open office plan for anyone with the right script.

## The Practical Reality

For those of us not running a frontier AI lab, the takeaway is sobering. If a company as sophisticated as Anthropic can be compromised in half a day, your internal security protocols probably need a serious audit. The practical implication is simple: stop assuming your internal network is inherently 'trusted' just because it is behind a firewall. If your administrative tools are accessible, they are vulnerable. It is time to start treating internal data with the same level of paranoia we usually reserve for public-facing servers.

## What to Watch Next

Moving forward, keep an eye on how these AI-driven red-teaming exercises evolve. We are likely to see a surge in companies hiring firms like Cathedral to test their defences against automated threats. The real question is whether the defensive side of the industry can keep pace. Are we going to see a new generation of 'AI-native' security tools designed to catch other AI agents, or are we just going to be locked in an endless arms race where the software is constantly trying to outsmart itself? I suspect it will be the latter, and it will be expensive.
