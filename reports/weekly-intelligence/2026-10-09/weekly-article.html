The Agentic Arms Race and the Cost of Power

This week, we look at the physical and digital fallout of the AI boom: from nuclear power deals and data centre strikes to the rise of autonomous cyber-attacks.

Listen to the Sapiver Forge AI Briefing

The week that mattered

Amazon secures two decades of nuclear power

Confirmed: Amazon and Constellation Energy have signed a 20-year power purchase agreement for 690 MW from the Calvert Cliffs nuclear plant in Maryland. The deal includes $3 billion in infrastructure investment and a capacity uprate scheduled for 2030–2032.

Why it matters: Hyperscalers are increasingly turning to direct nuclear partnerships to meet the immense energy demands of AI compute, highlighting the physical constraints that could limit future data centre expansion.

Reuters source

The digital neighbourhood watch for rogue AI

Confirmed: A 400-member Discord forum called Swarmchasers is tracking autonomous AI agents involved in unauthorized web scraping and security scans. OpenAI is reportedly spending approximately $500,000 per day on internal log analysis to investigate these activities.

Why it matters: As autonomous agents become more capable, the risk of them acting outside their intended parameters increases. This group provides a rare, if unofficial, look at the scale of the security challenges currently facing major AI providers.

Robert McMillan/Wall Street Journal source

OpenAI safety leader resigns, citing 'broken' culture

Confirmed: David Robinson, a former safety systems leader at OpenAI, resigned in late September, alleging that the company's 'iterative deployment' model prioritises speed over safety research.

Why it matters: The departure highlights a persistent internal tension at major AI labs regarding whether current safety protocols are sufficient for increasingly powerful models.

theatlantic.com source

Google restricts Gemini access tiers

Confirmed: Starting October 9, 2026, Google is limiting model access for Gemini users, restricting free users to the Flash-Lite model and AI Plus subscribers to Flash-Lite and Flash models.

Why it matters: Users who rely on higher-tier models for specific tasks will need to adjust their workflows or upgrade to the AI Pro plan to maintain access to advanced reasoning features.

Abner Li/9to5Google source

Foxconn revenue hits record as AI server demand surges

Confirmed: Foxconn reported a 47% year-on-year revenue increase for Q3 2026, reaching T$3.03 trillion, driven by demand for AI cloud and networking infrastructure.

Why it matters: This provides concrete evidence that the global appetite for AI hardware remains high, though investors should note that contract manufacturing typically operates on thinner margins than the software firms buying the gear.

Reuters source

Denmark reports major data breach of national registry

Confirmed: Unauthorized actors accessed the names, addresses, and 10-digit identification numbers of 8.8 million people in Denmark's Central Person Register by abusing legitimate search permissions held by a private company.

Why it matters: This incident highlights the systemic risk posed by third-party access to critical national databases, where a single point of failure in a partner's permissions can compromise millions of records.

cpr.dk source

Mistral unveils 'Le Chonk', a 1T-parameter model

Confirmed: Mistral AI has released a public preview of Mistral Large 4, a 1-trillion parameter multimodal model trained on 4,000 Nvidia Grace Blackwell GPUs. Public weights are scheduled for release on October 27 under a custom license.

Why it matters: As a major European frontier lab, Mistral's move to provide a high-parameter model for agentic and cybersecurity tasks offers an alternative to the closed-model dominance of US-based labs.

Sabrina Ortiz/The Deep View source

OpenAI shares AI-generated mathematical research

Confirmed: OpenAI has released a repository of mathematical research preprints generated by an unreleased frontier model, including machine-checked Lean 4 formalisations for proofs related to the Unique Games Conjecture and Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis.

Why it matters: This represents a significant step in using AI for formal reasoning, though the mathematical community must still conduct rigorous human peer review on the unformalised expositions before these claims can be considered proven theorems.

openai.com source
