Today's briefing covers the rising cost of AI infrastructure, the ongoing shadow war for semiconductor dominance, and the persistent security gaps in the AI supply chain.

business · Financial Times

AI server suppliers demand double-digit price hikes on signed contracts

What happened: Major AI server vendors including Dell, HPE, Lenovo, and Supermicro are demanding price increases of 25% to 45% on hardware orders months after contracts were signed. The hikes are attributed to a 400% increase in memory component costs since early 2024.

Why it matters: Fixed-price infrastructure planning is becoming increasingly difficult for enterprises, as evidenced by XTX Markets suing Dell over a demanded $62M–$70M price increase on a $549M order.

Our read: Hardware OEMs are passing the volatility of the memory market directly to customers, effectively shifting the risk of supply chain inflation onto the buyer after the deal is done.

technology · New York Times

Intelligence officials detail Chinese state-backed chip acquisition campaign

What happened: US, Asian, and European intelligence officials report that China’s Ministry of State Security established a specialized working group to circumvent US export restrictions and acquire advanced Nvidia AI chips. The Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs has denied these espionage allegations.

Why it matters: The campaign highlights the extreme lengths state actors are going to in order to bypass Western semiconductor export controls, complicating the global supply chain for high-end AI hardware.

Our read: The persistence of these efforts suggests that export controls are creating a high-stakes black market, with intelligence agencies acting as facilitators for industrial acquisition.

developer · Dan Goodin/Ars Technica

Security researchers find API keys leaked by AI routing services

What happened: A study using 2 million canary tokens found that sensitive API keys and request payloads were repeatedly logged and stored by multi-tenant AI routing services, even when those services advertised 'Zero Data Retention' policies.

Why it matters: The findings indicate that 'Zero Data Retention' claims are often undermined by downstream infrastructure logging and third-party API pipeline leaks, leaving developer credentials exposed.

Our read: Privacy could support in the AI supply chain are currently more aspirational than architectural; developers should treat these routing services as inherently leaky.

ai · Mike Pearl/Gizmodo

Tesla releases FSD v15 with 'Tesla Reality View'

What happened: Tesla has begun rolling out FSD v15, which introduces a 3D visualization system generated in real time from camera feeds. The system remains a Level 2 'Supervised' driver-assist feature requiring constant human attention.

Why it matters: While the visualization is a technical shift from traditional game-engine rendering, Tesla's internal safety claims lack independent audit, and the system remains a driver-assist tool, not an autonomous one.

Our read: Tesla is prioritising the 'feel' of the AI's perception, but the underlying safety reliance remains firmly on the human driver.
