After a global Microsoft outage and fresh scrutiny on semiconductor supply chains, the tech sector is catching its breath. Today, we look at the fallout from the Microsoft disruption, Nvidia’s complex relationship with Beijing, and a wave of new funding for AI infrastructure and governance.

lead · Bloomberg

Microsoft 365 Back Online After Global Outage

What happened: Microsoft confirmed that all flagship 365 applications, including Teams and Outlook, were fully restored by Saturday, November 14, following a multi-day outage that impacted over 70 countries. The company attributed the disruption to a secure-connection authentication bottleneck and server availability constraints.

Why it matters: The outage paralysed critical infrastructure, including government and healthcare operations, highlighting the fragility of relying on a single, centralised authentication layer for global enterprise workflows.

Our read: While Microsoft has ruled out malicious activity, the scale of the disruption suggests that even minor authentication bottlenecks can trigger cascading failures across modern, tightly coupled cloud ecosystems.

Nvidia’s Difficult Path to Beijing’s Inner Circle

What happened: Financial Times reporting indicates that former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang struggled to secure direct access to top Chinese officials, such as Vice Premier Ding Xuexiang, who are responsible for the country's chip self-sufficiency campaign.

Why it matters: This lack of high-level access underscores the growing geopolitical friction surrounding semiconductor trade, as Beijing prioritises domestic alternatives over established foreign suppliers.

Our read: Nvidia’s two decades of operation in China appear insufficient to insulate the company from the increasingly opaque and nationalistic nature of Chinese industrial policy.

ai · Alice Tecotzky/Business Insider

WhiteMatter Raises $20M to Guard Enterprise AI

What happened: Startup WhiteMatter has closed a $20M Series A round to develop 'Guardian' agents designed to monitor and enforce policy on enterprise AI agents. The company claims its platform can manage millions of active guardians with low-latency decision speeds.

Why it matters: As businesses deploy more autonomous agents, the risk of 'rogue' behaviour or unauthorised data access grows, creating a new market for automated oversight tools.

Our read: The rise of 'agent-on-agent' security suggests that the industry is shifting from simple permission controls to active, real-time governance as the primary method for managing AI risk.

technology · John Reynolds/Tech.eu

Tendrils Compute Secures $30M for Parallel CPU Architecture

What happened: UK-based Tendrils Compute has raised $30M led by Benchmark to develop a general-purpose processor featuring thousands of parallel cores based on interaction nets. The startup is also building an open-source programming language, Vine, to support the architecture.

Why it matters: Designing a new CPU architecture from scratch is a high-stakes endeavour; if successful, it could offer an alternative to traditional serial processing for specific compute-heavy tasks.

Our read: The investment reflects a broader venture interest in 'post-Moore' hardware designs that attempt to solve the efficiency bottlenecks inherent in current chip architectures.
