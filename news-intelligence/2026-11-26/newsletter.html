OpenAI corrects billing error, effectively tightening GPT-6.2 usage caps

What happened: OpenAI has corrected a billing error that incorrectly discounted usage for its GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol models. As a result, paying users will now reach their usage caps 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol than they did during the launch week.

Why it matters: For power users and businesses relying on these models, the change represents a significant reduction in the effective allowance per subscription, despite OpenAI's claims regarding the models' token efficiency.

Our read: The correction highlights the gap between initial launch-day expectations and the actual operational costs of running newer, more resource-intensive models.

Anthropic researcher compresses formal proof of Riemann Hypothesis

What happened: Anthropic researcher Luke Sernau has released a compact, open-source Lean 4 repository that reduces a 17-million-line formal proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis to approximately 76,000 lines. The proof has passed standard Lean kernel validation.

Why it matters: This is a notable step in AI-assisted mathematics, demonstrating that machine-generated proofs can be distilled into more manageable, verifiable forms without losing logical integrity.

Our read: While the proof is logically consistent within the Lean environment, the real test remains the ongoing effort by mathematicians to translate this into a human-readable format.

business · Financial Times

Institutional investors offload 2021-era VC stakes to fund AI bets

What happened: Institutional investors, including pension funds and university endowments, are selling their positions in 2021-era venture capital funds on the secondary market at steep discounts. The liquidity generated is being redirected toward new AI-focused technology funds.

Why it matters: This trend signals a broader reallocation of capital as institutional LPs attempt to exit the high-valuation vintage of 2021 to chase the current wave of AI investment.

Our read: The willingness to accept knockdown prices suggests that these investors view the potential upside of current AI-focused funds as worth the immediate loss on their legacy portfolios.

New York Times restricts Photoshop 2027 updates for staff photographers

What happened: The New York Times has directed contributing photographers to avoid updating to Adobe Photoshop 2027 (v28.0.0+) due to concerns over automated generative AI features that operate without explicit prompts and embed C2PA metadata.

Why it matters: The move highlights the tension between new AI-powered creative tools and the strict provenance requirements necessary for maintaining journalistic integrity.

Our read: Institutional resistance to these features is likely to grow as media organisations prioritise image authenticity over the convenience of automated editing tools.
