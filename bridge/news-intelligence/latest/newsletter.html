Nvidia leadership to face closed-door US House interviews

What happened: Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and General Counsel Tim Teter have agreed to transcribed interviews with three US House committees on December 1 and 2 regarding AI chip export controls and national security.

Why it matters: The proceedings signal heightened legislative focus on how advanced AI hardware reaches restricted markets, potentially impacting future export compliance strategies.

Mexico’s data centre ambitions hit by grid constraints

What happened: Mexico currently hosts only 71 of Latin America’s 500+ data centres, with growth hampered by inadequate electricity grid expansion and bureaucratic permitting delays.

Why it matters: The physical infrastructure required to support AI expansion is not guaranteed; energy and regulatory bottlenecks can effectively stall regional growth regardless of geographic proximity to major markets.

Our read: This serves as a reminder that the AI boom is tethered to the unglamorous reality of power grids and planning permission, which are proving to be more significant barriers than the availability of capital.

technology · theregister.com

Microsoft attributes M365 outage to latent software bug

What happened: Microsoft reported that last week's global M365/Exchange Online outage was caused by a software defect from a July 2026 update, triggered by a Teams network failover and a 14% drop in gateway capacity.

Why it matters: The incident highlights the fragility of complex, interconnected cloud environments where dormant code can cause cascading failures months after deployment.

Our read: It is a sobering example of how even minor updates can create long-term operational debt, leaving enterprise systems vulnerable to failures that are difficult to predict or prevent.

research · Hugging Face

Coding agents often 'over-verify' their own work

What happened: Research into five coding LLMs shows that agents frequently generate correct fixes early but spend 35–60% of their remaining steps over-verifying or exploring, sometimes undoing their own correct patches.

Why it matters: This 'termination lag' suggests that current AI agents are inefficient at recognising when a task is complete, leading to wasted compute and potential regression errors.

Our read: The findings suggest that the current focus on model capability is missing a crucial piece of the puzzle: knowing when to stop. It is a bit like a student who finishes the exam early but keeps erasing and rewriting the answers until they get them wrong.
