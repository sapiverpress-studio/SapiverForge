OpenAI has just dropped two new models, GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol. If you are a developer, you might be wondering if your workflow is about to get a serious upgrade or just a new set of headaches. Astra is being pitched for deep reasoning and agentic coding, while Sol is aimed at those multi-step, routine coding tasks. It is a classic 'good cop, bad cop' setup, though in this case, the jury is still very much out on whether either of them is actually ready to take the wheel.

So, what are we actually looking at? OpenAI has split its focus here. Astra is the heavy lifter, designed for complex, agentic coding tasks where deep reasoning is supposedly the star of the show. Then there is Sol, which is the more focused sibling, optimised for those repetitive, multi-step coding workflows that usually eat up your afternoon. On paper, it sounds like a sensible division of labour. But as anyone who has spent time with these models knows, the gap between a marketing brief and a functional IDE is often wider than it looks.

Early feedback from the developer community has been, let us say, characteristically honest. While the models are new, the reports are already showing a split. The main issue cropping up isn't about raw intelligence or the ability to write a function; it is about instruction-following. Specifically, users are finding that Astra—the 'smart' one—is proving surprisingly difficult to steer. When you give it a complex task, it seems to have a mind of its own, which is exactly the opposite of what you want when you are trying to automate a professional coding environment.

This matters because we are moving into the era of 'agentic' workflows. We don't just want a model to write a snippet of code; we want it to go off, figure out the dependencies, run the tests, and fix the errors. But if the model cannot follow instructions reliably, that entire vision falls apart. The bottleneck here isn't the model's ability to reason; it is its ability to stay on the leash. If you have to spend more time correcting the agent than you would have spent writing the code yourself, the productivity gain is effectively zero.

If you are thinking about integrating these into your daily stack, proceed with caution. The practical reality is that these systems are currently highly sensitive to how they are prompted. You cannot just throw a vague instruction at Astra and expect a perfect result. You are going to need to be incredibly precise with your steering. Think of it less like a junior developer you can delegate to, and more like a very fast, very literal intern who needs a detailed manual for every single step.

The big question now is whether OpenAI can patch these steering issues through fine-tuning or if this is just the current limit of the architecture. Keep an eye on how the community develops 'system prompts' or wrapper tools designed to constrain these models. If we start seeing reliable, third-party frameworks that successfully rein in Astra’s tendency to wander off-script, then we might actually have something useful. Until then, treat these as experimental tools rather than production-ready replacements for your current workflow.

It is a familiar story: more power, more complexity, and a new set of quirks to manage. Whether Astra and Sol become essential tools or just another pair of models that require too much babysitting remains to be seen. I will be watching to see if the next update fixes the steering, or if we are just going to have to get better at talking to our code-writing robots. Thanks for listening.
