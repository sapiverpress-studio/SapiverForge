Ramp hits $60B valuation as fintech spending stays hot

What happened: Corporate spend platform Ramp has closed a $1.85 billion funding round led by Dragoneer, valuing the company at $60 billion. The firm also confirmed it has reached $2 billion in annualized revenue with positive cash flow.

Why it matters: The valuation underscores continued investor appetite for AI-integrated financial operations platforms, even as the broader market remains cautious.

Our read: Crossing the $2 billion revenue mark while maintaining positive cash flow is a significant operational milestone that likely helped justify the premium valuation in a competitive fintech landscape.

OpenAI engineer arrested over hidden recording device

What happened: Former OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan was arrested by the FBI on October 9 after allegedly placing a hidden audio recording device in an executive conference room. Court filings reveal he had booked one-way tickets to Saudi Arabia for himself and family members shortly before his arrest.

Why it matters: While authorities have found no evidence of data or model theft, the incident highlights the physical security risks inherent in high-stakes AI research environments.

Our read: The incident is a reminder that even the most sophisticated AI labs are vulnerable to traditional, low-tech espionage tactics, regardless of their digital security posture.

technology · Dan Goodin/Ars Technica

Supermarket refrigeration hack causes mass spoilage

What happened: A cyberattack on legacy Danfoss refrigeration controllers forced roughly 700 French supermarkets into defrost mode, causing significant inventory loss and triggering electrical fires. The attack exploited known vulnerabilities in end-of-life devices that were left exposed to the internet without patches.

Why it matters: This incident demonstrates the real-world physical consequences of failing to secure legacy industrial IoT hardware, turning a digital vulnerability into a multi-million euro problem.

Our read: It is a stark example of how 'smart' infrastructure can become a liability when basic maintenance and network isolation are neglected for years.

business · Debby Wu/Bloomberg

TSMC sets terms for Musk's 'Terafab' project

What happened: TSMC CEO C.C. Wei confirmed on the company's Q3 earnings call that they are in discussions regarding Elon Musk's 'Terafab' project in Texas. Wei explicitly stated that any participating wafer fab must be operated by TSMC, countering Musk's previous suggestion of a sublease arrangement.

Why it matters: The statement highlights the power dynamic in semiconductor manufacturing, where the expertise required to run a modern fab remains a significant barrier to entry for even the largest tech companies.

Our read: TSMC is clearly signalling that it has no intention of relinquishing operational control, regardless of who owns the land or the building.
