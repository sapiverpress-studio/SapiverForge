Fractile hits $7B valuation as UK chip sector heats up

What happened: George Hammond/Financial Times reports, citing people familiar with the matter, that london-based AI chip startup Fractile has raised $720 million in a funding round led by Thrive Capital and Founders Fund, pushing its valuation to $7 billion.

Why it matters: The jump from a $1.1 billion valuation in May suggests significant investor appetite for custom semiconductor hardware tailored specifically for AI inference.

Our read: This is a substantial vote of confidence in the UK’s ability to produce competitive hardware, though the rapid valuation increase reflects the broader, high-stakes scramble for compute infrastructure.

Audit highlights gap in Anthropic’s vulnerability disclosures

What happened: A seven-month audit by VulnCheck found that while Anthropic’s internal ledger lists 37,966 findings, only 7,349 have been made public and 217 marked as fixed.

Why it matters: The discrepancy between vendor-reported safety metrics and independent vulnerability tracking underscores the difficulty in verifying the effectiveness of automated AI bug-hunting tools.

Our read: It is a reminder that internal disclosure ledgers are not the same as public security transparency; until these metrics align, independent scrutiny remains the only way to gauge actual progress.

research · deepmind.google

DeepMind uses AI to verify Signal protocol security

What happened: Google DeepMind’s AlphaProof Code framework has generated over 6.5 million lines of machine-checked proofs to verify the security properties of the Signal protocol's machine code.

Why it matters: This represents a significant milestone in using AI for formal verification, though it required 26,000 lines of human-written specifications and did not uncover new vulnerabilities.

Our read: It is an impressive technical feat, but it remains a research-grade tool rather than a plug-and-play solution for general software security.

Indie game developers face AI backlash

What happened: Game developer House House confirmed using LLMs to fix a rendering bug in 'Big Walk', prompting 19 Independent Games Festival jurors to decline voting for entries using generative AI.

Why it matters: The incident highlights the growing friction between practical AI utility in development and the creative community's stance on generative tools.

Our read: Even when AI is used for mundane debugging rather than creative output, the industry remains deeply divided on where to draw the line.
