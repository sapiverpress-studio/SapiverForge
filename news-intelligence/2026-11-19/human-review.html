Sapiver Forge Daily Brief

Anthropic hits the public markets, the UK Home Office loses a secrecy battle over encryption, and the European Commission deals with the fallout of an AI-powered cyberattack. It is a busy morning for the intersection of policy, security, and high-stakes finance.

lead · anthropic.com

Anthropic debuts on Nasdaq at $83 per share

What happened: Anthropic has priced its IPO at $83.00 per share, raising approximately $81.3 billion and valuing the company at roughly $1.5 trillion. Strategic partners Google and Nvidia have committed an additional $26 billion in non-voting shares.

Why it matters: This is one of the largest AI-sector public listings to date, marking a significant shift in how capital markets value foundational AI research and infrastructure.

Our read: The massive valuation reflects intense investor appetite for AI, though the real test begins as the stock faces its first days of public trading volatility.

technology · Tim Bradshaw/Financial Times

UK tribunal forces Home Office to admit Apple encryption notice

What happened: The Investigatory Powers Tribunal has ruled that the UK Home Office can no longer use 'Neither Confirm Nor Deny' rules to keep secret a technical capability notice served on Apple regarding encrypted iCloud data.

Why it matters: The ruling removes a layer of state secrecy surrounding how the government attempts to compel companies to bypass encryption, though the legality of the notice itself remains unresolved.

Our read: This is a rare win for transparency in a space where the government typically relies on opaque legal instruments to exert pressure on tech platforms.

business · Gerardo Fortuna/Politico

European Commission systems disrupted by AI-assisted cyberattack

What happened: The European Commission confirmed a cyberattack that disrupted internal services, including payroll and translation, for over 10 days. CERT-EU identified that attackers used ARTEX, an open-source AI penetration testing framework, to probe for vulnerabilities.

Why it matters: The incident demonstrates how readily available AI tools are being repurposed by threat actors to automate the discovery of security weaknesses in large-scale institutional networks.

Our read: While the Commission reports no evidence of data exfiltration yet, the use of AI frameworks to sustain a 10-day disruption highlights a growing challenge for public sector cybersecurity.

ai · blog.google

Google launches Gemini 4 Neon for enterprise coding

What happened: Google has released Gemini 4 Neon, a new model positioned between Carbon and Hydrogen, specifically targeting enterprise coding and reasoning tasks. It is now available via Google AI Pro, Antigravity, and GitHub Copilot.

Why it matters: The model aims to lower per-task token costs for developers, though performance claims are currently based on vendor-reported benchmarks.

Our read: Google is clearly trying to squeeze more efficiency out of its model family to better compete for the enterprise developer wallet.
