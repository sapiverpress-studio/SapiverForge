The Cost of Certainty

The week that mattered

Microsoft 365 Back Online After Global Outage

Confirmed: Microsoft confirmed that all flagship 365 applications, including Teams and Outlook, were fully restored by Saturday, November 14, following a multi-day outage that impacted over 70 countries. The company attributed the disruption to a secure-connection authentication bottleneck and server availability constraints.

Why it matters: The outage paralysed critical infrastructure, including government and healthcare operations, highlighting the fragility of relying on a single, centralised authentication layer for global enterprise workflows.

Bloomberg source

SpaceX achieves first orbital upper-stage tower catch

Confirmed: SpaceX successfully recovered the Starship upper stage (Ship 42) using the launch tower's 'chopstick' arms at Starbase on 14 November 2026. The mission's booster, however, aborted its catch attempt due to a low-fuel condition and splashed down in the Gulf of Mexico.

Why it matters: This marks a major milestone for rapid reusability in heavy-lift aerospace, though the failed booster catch highlights the ongoing reliability challenges of high-cadence flight operations.

spacex.com source

Autonomous AI framework turns security patches into exploits

Confirmed: Google's Threat Intelligence Group has documented the first in-the-wild use of an autonomous AI framework that analyzes software security patches to synthesize and execute exploits without human intervention.

Why it matters: This marks a shift from AI-assisted hacking to fully autonomous offensive operations, potentially shrinking the window between a vendor releasing a patch and an attacker weaponising it.

Duncan Riley/SiliconANGLE source

Nvidia leadership to face closed-door US House interviews

Confirmed: Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and General Counsel Tim Teter have agreed to transcribed interviews with three US House committees on December 1 and 2 regarding AI chip export controls and national security.

Why it matters: The proceedings signal heightened legislative focus on how advanced AI hardware reaches restricted markets, potentially impacting future export compliance strategies.

Garrett Downs/CNBC source

OpenAI launches GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol

Confirmed: OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra, designed for agentic coding and deep reasoning, alongside GPT-6.2 Sol, which is optimized for multi-step routine coding workflows. Early feedback from developers indicates mixed performance, specifically regarding instruction-following and steering difficulties in the Astra model.

Why it matters: As these models move toward agentic workflows, their reliability in following complex instructions becomes the primary bottleneck for practical integration into professional coding environments.

openai.com source

Internal docs reveal Meta's school-targeting strategy

Confirmed: Internal documents from 2024 detail Meta's 'Project Gryffindor,' an initiative that used school directories and notifications to drive teen user acquisition on Instagram. While Meta has publicly framed its school partnerships as safety and anti-bullying initiatives, the documents highlight explicit commercial growth targets.

Why it matters: The discrepancy between public safety messaging and internal growth strategies raises questions about the transparency of platform engagement tactics involving minors.

Katie McQue/NPR source

Anthropic's IPO haul hits $93.5B

Confirmed: Anthropic's underwriters have fully exercised their overallotment option, raising an additional $12.2 billion and bringing the total gross proceeds from the public offering to $93.5 billion.

Why it matters: This confirms the full scale of the largest IPO in history, cementing the massive capital commitment behind the company despite subsequent market volatility.

Ashley Capoot/CNBC source

US restricts remote access to advanced AI chips

Confirmed: The US Department of Commerce has issued an interim rule requiring licenses for data centers globally that provide significant processing power to entities in arms-embargoed countries, while adding new export requirements for several Southeast Asian and South American nations.

Why it matters: This move attempts to close the 'remote access' loophole, where companies could bypass physical export bans by renting compute capacity from foreign data centers.
