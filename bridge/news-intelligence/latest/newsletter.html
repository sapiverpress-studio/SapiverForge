Tech stocks stumble as AI trade cools

What happened: US tech stocks saw a sharp decline on November 25, 2026, with the Nasdaq 100 falling over 4%. Major AI-exposed equities including Nvidia, Anthropic, Broadcom, and Micron experienced significant single-day losses.

Why it matters: This downturn reflects a broader shift in investor sentiment and macroeconomic recalibration rather than specific operational failures at these companies.

Our read: The market is showing signs of fatigue after a long period of AI-focused growth, suggesting investors are becoming more sensitive to valuation and sector-wide volatility.

Restricted Nvidia hardware prices surge in Shenzhen

What happened: Black-market vendors in Shenzhen are reportedly offering restricted Nvidia Blackwell B300 server rigs for $3M to $3.4M, an increase from the $2M asking price reported two months ago.

Why it matters: The rising cost suggests that despite US export restrictions, demand for high-end AI hardware remains robust in restricted markets, though the physical authenticity of these units remains unverified.

Our read: The price hike highlights the persistent difficulty of enforcing hardware export controls when high-value, compact technology is involved.

developer · Anthropic

Anthropic migrates API to Rust

What happened: Anthropic has migrated its primary Messages API routing service from Python to Rust, reporting a 40%–50% reduction in CPU consumption and a 10%–20% improvement in time-to-first-token latency.

Why it matters: The migration demonstrates a practical application of AI-assisted coding, as the team reportedly used Claude Code to generate the majority of the Rust implementation.

Our read: While the performance gains are impressive for infrastructure, they are limited to the API proxy layer and do not change the speed of the underlying model generation.

research · alphaXiv

OpenAI preprint targets matrix multiplication exponent

What happened: OpenAI has published an un-peer-reviewed preprint claiming to prove the optimal square matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 2.25.

Why it matters: As an asymptotic complexity result, this theoretical proof does not provide immediate speed improvements for current GPU hardware or existing software libraries.

Our read: It is a significant theoretical claim, but until it undergoes rigorous peer review and verification by the broader mathematical community, it remains an academic proposal rather than a practical breakthrough.
