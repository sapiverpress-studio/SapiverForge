# The Cloud Under Fire: Yandex's Triple Strike

Three data centres in four days. We look at the physical reality of digital infrastructure as Yandex Cloud faces a series of coordinated strikes.

## The scale of the disruption

The numbers here are significant. We are talking about the loss of a 50-megawatt facility, which is a substantial chunk of computing power by any standard. This latest incident is the third of its kind in just four days. According to Yandex’s own status reports, this has triggered a cascade of failures, disrupting over 80 different cloud services and consumer applications. These aren't just niche internal tools; they are platforms that underpin daily digital life across Russia and the CIS markets. When a hub of this size goes dark, the ripple effect is immediate and messy.

## A pattern, not a coincidence

It is difficult to look at three strikes in four days and call them isolated events. The speed and precision of these attacks suggest a coordinated strategy aimed at degrading regional digital capacity. This isn't just about knocking a website offline for an hour; it is a systematic attempt to erode the infrastructure that keeps modern enterprise APIs and consumer services running. Whoever is behind this clearly understands that in the modern economy, you don't need to bomb a factory to stop production—you just need to cut the digital nervous system.

## The fragility of centralisation

The real issue here is the inherent vulnerability of centralised digital services. We have spent the last decade pushing everything into massive, hyper-scale data centres because they are efficient, cost-effective, and easy to manage. But efficiency has a hidden cost: it creates a single point of failure. When you aggregate that much computing power into a few key locations, you are essentially painting a target on the back of your entire digital ecosystem. It is a classic engineering trade-off, and right now, the trade-off is looking increasingly expensive.

## What this means for the rest of us

For anyone relying on these services, the practical implication is a sudden, sharp return to the reality of 'offline-first' planning. If your business or your daily routine depends on a cloud provider, you have to assume that the connection is not guaranteed. We are seeing a shift where the physical location of your data is no longer just a regulatory checkbox—it is a survival metric. If you are building systems today, you have to ask yourself: what happens when the building housing my server is no longer there?

## What to watch next

The big question now is how Yandex and other regional providers respond. Are they going to attempt to decentralise their infrastructure, or will they double down on hardening the facilities they have left? We should be watching for any signs of service migration or emergency failover protocols being triggered. More importantly, keep an eye on the broader market reaction. If this becomes the new normal for regional cloud infrastructure, we might see a very rapid, very expensive rethink of how we distribute digital assets in conflict-prone areas.
