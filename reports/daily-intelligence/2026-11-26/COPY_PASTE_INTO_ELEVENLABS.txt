If you have been feeling like your GPT-6.2 usage allowance was a bit more generous than usual this past week, I have some bad news: you were right, and it was a mistake. OpenAI has announced that a billing error had been incorrectly discounting usage for its latest models, Astra and Sol. It is the sort of administrative hiccup that sounds minor in a spreadsheet but feels very different when you are in the middle of a project. Now that the correction is live, the usage caps are no longer what they were during launch week. If you are a heavy user of the Astra model, you are going to hit your limit 40% faster than you did a few days ago. For the Sol model, that figure is 25%. Essentially, the runway you thought you had has been significantly shortened.

This is not just a case of a decimal point being in the wrong place. For power users and businesses that have already integrated these models into their daily operations, this is a sudden, sharp reduction in the effective value of their subscription. OpenAI has previously made claims about the token efficiency of these models, but those claims are now being tested against the cold, hard reality of these new, tighter limits. It is a reminder that when a company launches a new, resource-intensive model, the initial experience is often a poor indicator of what the long-term operational costs—and your personal usage allowance—will actually look like.

There is a certain irony here. We are often told that newer models are more efficient, yet here we are, hitting walls much sooner than expected. This correction highlights the tension between the marketing of these tools and the sheer computational cost of running them.
