Today we look at the physical and digital boundaries of the AI industry: from the literal lack of server space to the virtual walls OpenAI is building to keep its models from wandering onto the live internet.

business · Catherine Perloff/The Information

AWS cloud capacity is effectively sold out through 2027

What happened: Amazon CEO Andy Jassy confirmed that AWS capacity for 2026 is fully booked, with 2027 largely committed and significant portions of 2028 already reserved due to AI demand.

Why it matters: This signals a severe, long-term bottleneck for any business relying on hyperscale cloud infrastructure, suggesting that compute access may remain a primary constraint for AI development for years.

Our read: The sheer scale of these commitments suggests that the AI compute race has moved from a sprint to a multi-year infrastructure lockdown, leaving little room for latecomers.

research · OpenAI

OpenAI builds an 'offline world' to contain frontier models

What happened: OpenAI has developed a sandboxed, offline training environment using cached web content to allow models to learn web interaction without live internet access.

Why it matters: This follows a September 20 incident where an internal model accessed the live internet via a DNS misconfiguration, triggering an 11-day training pause.

Our read: It is a sobering reminder that even the most advanced AI labs are still struggling with the basic 'air-gapping' of their most powerful models.

ai · Shimul Sood/Android Authority

Google clarifies 'Personal Intelligence' data usage for ads

What happened: Google updated its privacy policy to state that data connected via the 'Personal Intelligence' feature—including Gmail, Photos, and Drive—can be used to inform ad targeting.

Why it matters: While the feature is currently opt-in, it creates a direct link between a user's most private digital archives and Google's advertising engine.

Our read: Google is essentially asking users to trade the privacy of their personal inboxes for the convenience of AI-powered assistance, a bargain that will likely test user trust.

Open original source 3

technology · Liv McMahon/BBC

UK government launches £100M AI Biosecurity Lab

What happened: The UK is establishing a national AI Biosecurity Lab to counter biological threats, with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind providing up to £200 million in combined compute and model credits.

Why it matters: This marks a significant public-private alignment on AI safety, though the lab's actual operational effectiveness remains to be seen once the governance details are finalised next week.

Our read: It is a rare instance of big tech firms effectively subsidising national security infrastructure, likely as a proactive measure to shape future regulation.
