Good morning. Today we are looking at the fragility of our cloud-dependent financial system, the scramble for compliant silicon, and the ongoing tug-of-war between regulators and platform giants.

lead · Bloomberg

Google Cloud outage hits UK banking

What happened: Bloomberg reports, citing people familiar with the matter, that A Google Cloud outage in the London region starting at 06:08 GMT on October 20 caused widespread service disruptions for Revolut, Emma, and services at Lloyds and Halifax. Google has not yet detailed the root cause of the network failures.

Why it matters: When the cloud sneezes, our digital wallets catch a cold. It serves as a stark reminder that even the most robust infrastructure has a single point of failure.

Our read: While Revolut confirmed funds remain safe, the reliance of multiple major financial institutions on a single cloud region highlights a concentration risk that regulators are increasingly watching.

business · Reuters

The $35B scramble for export-compliant chips

What happened: ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent have placed orders for over 3 million Nvidia RTX PRO 5500 GPUs, totalling more than $35 billion. Nvidia has capped 2027 production at 3 million units, meaning customers may only receive a fraction of their requested allocation.

Why it matters: US export controls are forcing a massive pivot in Chinese AI infrastructure toward workstation-grade silicon, creating a supply bottleneck that will likely persist well into 2027.

Our read: This isn't just about buying chips; it's a desperate attempt to maintain AI development momentum under strict regulatory constraints.

technology · Paul Sandle/Reuters

CMA takes aim at Apple's payment garden

What happened: The UK Competition and Markets Authority has proposed mandates requiring Apple to open its iPhone NFC technology to third-party payment apps and reduce platform access fees. The proposal is currently open for public consultation until November 17, 2026.

Why it matters: If finalised, this could fundamentally change how UK consumers use their iPhones for contactless payments, potentially ending Apple's exclusive control over the NFC chip.

Our read: Apple is predictably citing security concerns, but the CMA seems determined to treat the iPhone's payment ecosystem as a utility that needs to be opened up.

ai · redact.dev

Meta's hidden 'don't tell' AI prompts

What happened: Security researchers discovered hidden system prompts in WhatsApp help pages instructing support AI not to cite privacy frameworks like GDPR or recommend data protection authorities. Meta stated the visibility of these prompts was an unintentional rendering bug, which was patched on October 18.

Why it matters: It raises questions about how companies configure the 'guardrails' of their support bots and whether those instructions align with the transparency users expect.

Our read: It is a classic case of a company trying to keep its support bots from giving legal advice, but doing so in a way that looks suspiciously like avoiding accountability.
