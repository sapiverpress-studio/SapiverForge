lead · Bloomberg

Jensen Huang Resigns as Nvidia CEO

What happened: Nvidia founder Jensen Huang resigned on 12 November 2026 following disclosures regarding extramarital relationships. CFO Colette Kress has been appointed interim CEO, and Harvey C. Jones has been named the company's first-ever Chair of the Board.

Why it matters: Nvidia is the primary engine of the current AI boom; a leadership vacuum at this scale creates significant uncertainty for the entire AI supply chain and the company's extensive network of venture-backed partners.

Our read: The board's move to appoint an external chair suggests a rapid effort to stabilise governance, though the market's immediate 7–14% drop reflects deep investor anxiety about the company's future direction without its long-time architect.

business · cbsnews.com

United Airlines Blocks Autonomous AI Agents

What happened: United Airlines updated its policies on 11 November 2026 to prohibit third-party autonomous AI agents from searching, booking, or managing flight reservations. The airline cited security risks, potential credential sharing, and regulatory requirements for human interaction during check-in.

Why it matters: This is a significant operational pushback against the 'agentic' web, highlighting that even as AI becomes more capable, legacy industries may restrict access to protect security and regulatory compliance.

Our read: While this is currently an isolated policy, it sets a precedent for other highly regulated sectors to wall off their booking systems from autonomous agents if they cannot could support safety and fraud prevention.

developer · github.com

AWS Emulator Built Entirely by AI Agents

What happened: Amazon released the AWS Emulator on 11 November 2026, a tool for local testing of over 200 AWS services. Amazon stated that 100% of the codebase was written, tested, and maintained by autonomous AI agents.

Why it matters: This marks a notable milestone in software engineering, demonstrating that autonomous agents can now handle complex, large-scale infrastructure tooling, provided they operate within human-designed evaluation pipelines.

Our read: The release serves as a high-profile proof of concept for agentic development, though its utility remains limited to local unit testing rather than production-grade deployment.

ai · blog.google

Google Launches Gemini Decisions API

What happened: Google's new Gemini Decisions API, powered by the gemini-decision-1 model, evaluates inputs to provide calibrated probabilities for branching logic rather than generating open-ended text.

Why it matters: By focusing on structured decision-making rather than generative text, this tool offers developers a more reliable way to build programmatic logic into AI-driven applications.

Our read: This is a pragmatic move to address the 'hallucination' problem in agentic workflows by forcing the model to output probabilities for specific choices instead of unpredictable prose.

Practical takeaway

If you are building with AI agents, expect increasing friction with legacy platforms like United Airlines that prioritise security over automation. For developers, tools like the AWS Emulator and Gemini Decisions API suggest a shift toward more structured, agent-native infrastructure that prioritises reliability over open-ended generation.

What to watch next

Monitor how other airlines and travel platforms respond to the rise of autonomous booking agents, and keep an eye on whether Nvidia's interim leadership can maintain the company's venture-backed ecosystem during the search for a permanent CEO.
