business · Rhea Rose Abraham/Reuters

Character.AI files for Chapter 11 bankruptcy

What happened: Character Technologies Inc. filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on October 12, 2026, and has entered a $20 million stalking-horse agreement with Disney for specific IP and transition services. The company intends to keep its application online during the proceedings, though it faces a potential wind-down if the court-supervised auction fails to secure a buyer.

Why it matters: The move highlights the intense financial pressure on consumer-facing AI platforms, even those with significant user bases, as they struggle to convert engagement into sustainable business models.

Our read: Disney’s involvement suggests a strategic interest in the underlying IP rather than the operational business, setting a low floor for the valuation of a once-hyped AI startup.

business · Financial Times

Broadcom CEO’s 'casino' warning triggers chip stock sell-off

What happened: Broadcom CEO Hock Tan described speculative AI investment as a 'casino' during an event on October 12, 2026, contributing to a sharp decline in semiconductor stocks including Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. Broadcom simultaneously maintained its own growth outlook, projecting $230 billion in custom AI chip revenue by 2028.

Why it matters: The comments reflect growing investor anxiety regarding the actual return on investment for enterprise AI, which is now beginning to impact the valuations of major infrastructure providers.

Our read: The market reaction suggests that even infrastructure giants are not immune to the cooling sentiment surrounding the broader AI boom, as investors start to differentiate between hardware demand and speculative hype.

technology · Reuters

Nvidia’s China market share drops as Huawei gains ground

What happened: IDC data indicates Nvidia’s share of China’s data-center AI accelerator market fell to 34% in H1 2026, with shipments dropping 44% year-on-year to 678,000 units. During the same period, Huawei’s shipments rose to 572,000 units, capturing 28% of the market.

Why it matters: US export restrictions are effectively reshaping the competitive landscape in China, allowing domestic alternatives to fill the supply gap created by the absence of high-end Western silicon.

Our read: The data suggests that trade policy is successfully limiting Nvidia's reach, but it is also accelerating the maturation of a domestic Chinese AI hardware ecosystem.

research · Anthropic

Anthropic claims success in blocking model distillation

What happened: Anthropic reports that new 'preserved thinking' safeguards in its Claude Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5 models have reduced data harvesting via distillation attacks by 90%–99%. The company identified Zhipu AI and Moonshot AI as sources of unauthorized data harvesting.

Why it matters: Model distillation—where a smaller model is trained on the outputs of a larger, more capable one—is a primary method for competitors to replicate frontier model performance without the massive R&D costs.

Our read: While these safeguards may protect IP, they also introduce friction for legitimate developers, illustrating the difficult trade-off between model security and API usability.

technology · Nikkei Asia

NTT Data in talks for $6.3B Anthropic data center

What happened: NTT Data is in advanced negotiations to build a 500-megawatt data center for Anthropic in Osaka, Japan, with a projected cost of up to $6.3 billion. The parties aim to reach a final agreement by March 2027, with early operations potentially relying on on-site fuel cells due to local grid constraints.

Why it matters: The scale of this project underscores the massive physical infrastructure requirements of frontier AI labs as they seek to expand their compute footprint globally.

Our read: The reliance on on-site power generation highlights that the primary bottleneck for AI expansion is increasingly the availability of reliable, high-capacity energy infrastructure.
