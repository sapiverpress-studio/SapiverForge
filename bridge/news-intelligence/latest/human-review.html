Sapiver Forge Daily Brief

Today, we look at the extraordinary political theatre surrounding Nvidia's leadership, SpaceX's pivot to infrastructure landlord, and a new proposal for how we might actually measure AI progress.

lead · cnbc.com

Jensen Huang Reinstated as Nvidia CEO Following Presidential Intervention

What happened: Nvidia’s board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO on November 23, 2026, following direct communication from President Donald Trump. The company is now creating a Chief Compliance Officer role to report to its governance committee.

Why it matters: The move highlights the intense intersection of corporate governance, national security, and political pressure. With Huang set to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3, the company faces significant ongoing scrutiny regarding export controls.

Our read: The board's decision to create a new compliance role suggests an attempt to formalise oversight in response to intense bipartisan criticism, though the direct involvement of the White House in a private company's leadership shuffle is highly unusual.

business · New York Times

SpaceX Pivots to Compute Rental as AI Internal Efforts Stall

What happened: SpaceX is generating over half its revenue by leasing GPU capacity from its Colossus data centers to competitors, including Anthropic and Google. The company has reportedly disbanded or redirected several internal AI software projects.

Why it matters: It is a rare admission that the infrastructure business is currently more lucrative than the product business. For SpaceX, the shift suggests that the immediate returns from selling compute to rivals outweigh the current progress of their own internal AI development.

Our read: When a company decides it is more profitable to be the landlord than the tenant, it is a clear signal that the internal AI roadmap has hit a wall.

research · openai.com

OpenAI Proposes Standardised 'Perplexity' Metric for Industry Pacing

What happened: OpenAI has proposed that frontier AI labs adopt log-perplexity as a standardized metric to measure pretraining intelligence and establish voluntary thresholds for development.

Why it matters: Standardising how we measure 'intelligence' is notoriously difficult. While this proposal aims to create a shared language for safety, it remains a voluntary framework that does not account for the vast differences in pretraining data across labs.

Our read: It is a clever attempt to set the rules of the game, but until labs agree on how to compare metrics across different datasets, it remains a proposal rather than a solution.

ai · z.ai

Z.ai Announces 'Loop Transformer' Architecture

What happened: Z.ai has announced GLM-5.4, a 750B-parameter model that uses a 'Loop Transformer' architecture, where 28 weight-shared layers run for two iterations per forward pass.

Why it matters: Z.ai claims this architecture allows a 750B model to match the performance of 2T–3T parameter models. However, these are currently vendor claims without independent benchmark data or public model weights.

Our read: Efficiency gains are always welcome, but until we see independent evaluations, this is a technical claim that requires a healthy dose of scepticism.
