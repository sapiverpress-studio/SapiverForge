#### **Candidate [14]: Microsoft unveils Maia 300 custom AI inference chip**
* **What is confirmed:** Microsoft officially announced the **Maia 300**, its third-generation custom AI accelerator built on TSMC’s N2P process node with HBM4 memory. Microsoft stated that Maia accelerators will become its primary inference platform starting in 2027, with multi-gigawatt production deployments planned. Racks hold 96 accelerators in a 167-kW power envelope.
* **Important caveats:** Production scaling is targeted for 2027, not immediately available. Performance metrics (e.g., 40% higher token output per megawatt compared to leading GPU systems) are vendor-reported figures from Microsoft's technical paper and await independent third-party benchmarks. Microsoft is not opening Maia directly to cloud rental customers; it remains internal infrastructure.
* **Publication status:** **Suitable for publication today.** High-priority headline story for enterprise AI hardware and cloud strategy.

#### **Candidate [8]: Counterpoint Research: Global smartphone shipments drop 20% in Q3 amid memory shortage**
* **What is confirmed:** Preliminary Q3 2026 market data from Counterpoint Research shows global smartphone shipments fell **20% YoY**, hitting a 14-year low for the third quarter. The decline was heavily driven by soaring DRAM/NAND memory prices and supply diversion to AI data center buildouts. Samsung held the top spot at 23% market share, while Apple reached a Q3 record 22% share despite overall volume drops.
* **Important caveats:** Figures are preliminary industry market research estimates. Counterpoint notes that premium phone demand remained resilient, while budget and mid-range devices suffered the largest price increases and supply cuts.
* **Publication status:** **Suitable for publication today.** Key macro update on consumer tech hardware and the knock-on supply chain impact of AI infrastructure demand.

#### **Candidate [3]: QGIS bans AI-assisted code contributions (QEP 428)**
* **What is confirmed:** Maintainers of the open-source GIS software QGIS passed QGIS Enhancement Proposal 428 (QEP 428 / PR #387) to formally prohibit AI-generated or AI-assisted contributions. The decision replaces a previous "human-in-the-loop" tagging policy due to maintainer burnout and a influx of unmergeable AI pull requests.
* **Important caveats:** Core maintainers explicitly noted in project discussions that the ban acts as a strict moratorium baseline; exceptions (such as IDE autocomplete tools or language localization) may be evaluated and relaxed on a case-by-case basis as policy definitions refine.
* **Publication status:** **Suitable for publication today.** Strong case study in open-source developer governance and the operational strain of AI-generated code.
