SpaceX achieves first orbital upper-stage tower catch

What happened: SpaceX successfully recovered the Starship upper stage (Ship 42) using the launch tower's 'chopstick' arms at Starbase on 14 November 2026. The mission's booster, however, aborted its catch attempt due to a low-fuel condition and splashed down in the Gulf of Mexico.

Why it matters: This marks a major milestone for rapid reusability in heavy-lift aerospace, though the failed booster catch highlights the ongoing reliability challenges of high-cadence flight operations.

Our read: The successful upper-stage recovery is a significant engineering feat, but the booster's diversion serves as a reminder that even mature launch systems remain sensitive to real-time performance variables.

Google Project Zero accelerates vulnerability disclosure timeline

What happened: Google's Project Zero is moving to a 'Full X' disclosure policy, ending grace periods for vulnerability patches. The disclosure window will drop to 90 days immediately, 60 days in February 2027, and 30 days in January 2028.

Why it matters: Google cited the rise of LLM-assisted vulnerability discovery and automated patch reverse-engineering as the primary reasons for shortening the time software vendors have to fix security flaws.

Our read: The shift suggests that the speed at which attackers can weaponise disclosed vulnerabilities is outpacing traditional patch-management cycles, forcing a more aggressive security posture.

business · litnews.ai

DRAM price-fixing class action survives dismissal

What happened: A U.S. federal court has denied a motion to dismiss a class-action lawsuit alleging that Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron coordinated output cuts to inflate DRAM prices under the guise of pivoting to AI-focused memory.

Why it matters: The case targets the 'Big Three' memory makers, who control approximately 90% of the global DRAM market, potentially impacting the cost structure of AI hardware infrastructure.

Our read: While the court's decision allows the case to proceed, the plaintiffs still face a high evidentiary burden to prove explicit collusion, a hurdle that has led to the dismissal of similar cases in the past.

Nebuly raises $21M to quantify AI ROI

What happened: Enterprise AI analytics firm Nebuly has raised $21 million in Series A funding to scale its platform, which is designed to measure the financial impact and productivity gains of deployed AI agents.

Why it matters: As corporate AI adoption matures, the ability to move beyond pilot projects to measurable revenue impact is becoming a critical requirement for enterprise buyers.

Our read: The investment reflects a broader market shift where the focus is moving from 'AI capability' to 'AI accountability' as companies seek to justify their infrastructure spending.
