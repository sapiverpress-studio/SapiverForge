### **Candidate [14] / [17]: Trump Unveils Voluntary "White House Accord on Super Intelligence"**
* **Publisher / Source:** Reuters / `reuters.com`
* **Editorial Scope:** IN SCOPE (AI Policy / Regulation)
* **What is confirmed:** President Donald Trump released the "White House Accord on Super Intelligence: Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities" on September 29, 2026, following a White House meeting with tech executives. Signatories include Sundar Pichai (Google), Dario Amodei (Anthropic), Mark Zuckerberg (Meta), Greg Brockman (OpenAI), Elon Musk (xAI), and Jensen Huang (Nvidia). The agreement calls for four layers of self-regulation: internal controls, internal evaluation teams, third-party external auditors, and independent board oversight focused on cybersecurity, biosecurity, and system-hacking risks. Trump also instructed federal agencies to adopt the term "Super Intelligence" ("SI") in place of "AI".
* **Important caveats:** The pact is non-enforceable and purely voluntary. It rejects mandatory federal government guardrails in favor of industry self-policing.
* **Publication assessment:** **SUITABLE FOR PUBLICATION TODAY.** High-impact geopolitical and regulatory story directly shaping US AI policy.

### **Candidate [19]: UK Tribunal Revives £300M Apple & Amazon Antitrust Lawsuit**
* **Publisher / Source:** Reuters / `reuters.com`
* **Editorial Scope:** IN SCOPE (Tech Policy / Antitrust Litigation)
* **What is confirmed:** On September 28, 2026, the UK Competition Appeal Tribunal (CAT) ruled that part of a revived collective action against Apple and Amazon can move forward. The lawsuit alleges a 2018 agreement between the two tech giants restricted third-party merchants selling Apple and Beats products on Amazon's UK marketplace, artificially inflating prices. The marketplace portion of the lawsuit is valued between £289M and £306M ($383M–$405M).
* **Important caveats:** The CAT dismissed a broader claim that sought damages for purchases made directly through Apple stores or non-Amazon third-party retailers, deeming those theories speculative. Apple and Amazon both maintain the remaining claim is without merit and that the agreement aimed to counter counterfeit products.
* **Publication assessment:** **SUITABLE FOR PUBLICATION TODAY.** Highly relevant to the UK briefing given the local jurisdiction, substantial damages, and ongoing platform regulation themes.
